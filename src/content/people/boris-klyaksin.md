Boris Mikhailovich Klyaksin is a conservation scientist from Kazan who spent
eleven years studying paper, pigments and the consequences of putting a mug
next to either. His first major research project established that a supposedly
ancient brown wash was a recent tea spill. The resulting paper was accepted;
his request to list the kettle as a co-author was not.

He teaches the materials lab and seal clinic, helping students distinguish
what they can observe from what they would need to test. For the painting
assignments, he runs practical experiments in ink dilution, paper absorbency
and why the same stroke behaves differently on different surfaces.

Boris maintains a reference drawer labelled “Suspicious Browns” and a second
labelled “Browns Cleared of Suspicion”. Transfers between them require a written
reason. Bring him a scientific-sounding attribution claim and he will help you
turn it into a testable question. Bring him a wet painting and he will first
find somewhere sensible to put it.

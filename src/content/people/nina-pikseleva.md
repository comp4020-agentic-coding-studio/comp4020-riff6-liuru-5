Nina Sergeyevna Pikseleva grew up in Novosibirsk and moved from computer vision
into art history after a model confidently attributed every image with a grey
border to the same painter. The painter turned out to be a flatbed scanner.
She now treats suspiciously good results as the beginning of the investigation.

Nina is a postdoctoral researcher building stroke-level classifiers for
ink-and-brush paintings. In week 9 she leads the model audit: students test
what a classifier notices, what it overlooks and whether its confidence has
anything to do with being right. Her connection to the painting assignments
is practical: a model may spot a repeated visual pattern, but it cannot stand
in for your process record or establish who held the brush.

Her office contains a tiny certificate awarded to “Scanner No. 2, Master of
Landscape”. Her opening slide is more serious: a model that agrees with the
connoisseurs is not yet interesting, and one that disagrees with them is not
yet trustworthy. She expects students to investigate both outcomes, preferably
before announcing a discovery.

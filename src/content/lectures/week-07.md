"Suzhou pian" names a real, organised production system, not a scattering of
individual con artists: commercial workshops in and around Suzhou turning
out paintings in the styles and under the names of Tang Yin, Qiu Ying and
other famous masters, at a scale driven by a genuine "craze for antiquities"
among Ming and Qing collectors who wanted attributed paintings faster than
attributed painters could supply them. The label covers a wide range ---
crude pieces made for a low market alongside skilled ones good enough to
later enter the Qing imperial collection --- which is itself the point:
"forgery" here was a business with a supply chain, a quality gradient, and
customers who sometimes bought knowingly.

That reframes the whole toolkit built so far. Materials, brushwork, seals
and colophons aren't just tests applied to isolated fakes; they're the
instruments a market this large made necessary, because a discipline that
can't tell workshop production from an individual master's hand can't
function at the volume Suzhou was actually operating at.

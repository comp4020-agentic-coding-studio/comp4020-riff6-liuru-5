A colophon --- a later owner's or scholar's inscription, mounted beside or
after a painting --- is a documentary layer distinct from the object itself:
a claim about the work, made by someone with a reputation to spend on being
right. Cahill situated this kind of authentication within kaozheng, the
Qing tradition of rigorous textual criticism applied to classical texts: the
same evidentiary standards --- who is claiming this, when, on what basis,
and does it survive comparison with everything else known --- carried over
from verifying a text to verifying a painting.

A colophon is evidence about the painting, not proof of it: a later owner's
confident attribution, written in good faith, is only as reliable as that
owner's own eye, and history has several colophons confidently attached to
the wrong painter. This week treats colophons and catalogue entries as a
chain that has to be read critically at every link, not accepted at the
strongest-sounding one.

## Outline

- what a colophon is, and why it's a claim rather than a fact
- kaozheng as a method: provenance chased and cross-checked like a textual

- a case where a spurious inscription held for two centuries before being
  overturned
- what this closes off before the break: materials, brushwork, seals and

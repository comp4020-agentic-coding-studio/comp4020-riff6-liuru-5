Style can be imitated; a sheet of paper's fibre composition is much harder
to fake convincingly, because a forger has to source period-correct material
before a single brushstroke goes down, and most don't. Paper and silk carry
physical evidence a connoisseur's eye never sees directly: fibre type and
weave under magnification, the specific mineral and organic content of ink
and pigment, wear and repair patterns consistent (or not) with an object's
claimed age.

None of this is decisive on its own --- a forger working from period paper
stock, which happened often enough in the Suzhou pian workshops, defeats a
purely material argument --- but it is the one class of evidence in this
course that doesn't depend on anyone's trained eye at all. That is exactly
why it's taught first: it gives you a check against your own stylistic
judgement in weeks 4 and 6, rather than a judgement that has to trust itself.

Week 11 argued that "authentic" is a working distinction, not a fact waiting
to be dug up. That is a reason to hold a verdict carefully, not a reason to
stop giving one: a museum still has to decide what a wall label says, a
collector still has to decide what to pay, and a scholar still has to decide
what claim to put their name behind in print. The whole semester has been
building the evidence a verdict like that should rest on; this lecture is
about the last piece, which is presentation --- saying plainly what you
believe, how confident you are, and exactly what would change your mind.

The final project brings that reasoning back to your own hand. Present your
revised painting beside your first study, explain the research that informed
it, and defend what the comparison does and does not establish. Your dossier's
historical painting supplies evidence to discuss; the generated landscape
supplies a shared model to paint. Neither resemblance nor skill at copying
establishes historical authorship. The defense tests whether you can keep
those distinctions clear when the room questions a specific choice.

## The brief

> Paint from *Pine Pavilion after Rain*. Make a study and a revised painting that show what
> a semester of looking, making and researching has changed in your hand.

Retain the reference's pine, pavilion, stream and layered mountain composition.
Begin with a preliminary study and a sheet of stroke experiments. Then choose
three passages to revise deliberately in your final painting: for example,
the pine's branching rhythm, the rock texture or the transition into mist.
You may change the season, washes or emphasis, provided you explain those
choices and the relationship to the reference remains visible.

Use ink or watercolour on A4 or larger paper, or manually painted digital work
at least 1500 pixels tall. Paint both works yourself; do not trace, filter the reference or use generated
imagery as your submission. Keep photographs or digital layers as process evidence. Technical polish alone does not earn the highest marks:
show attentive observation, purposeful revision and an honest account of limits.

## Connect making to research

Return to one clue you used in the first assignment. Test whether deliberately
reproducing that effect changes how much you trust it as a sign of authorship.

Use your dossier's historical painting as a second point of comparison.
Identify two specific details in its cited reproduction that informed your
choices, showing crops and explaining what you learned. Do not claim that
imitating those details authenticates either your painting or the generated
reference. Discuss what reproductions leave unknown about physical materials,
and what evidence would change your dossier's verdict.

## What you submit

Submit a PDF portfolio with your final painting, the first study, at least
three process images of the new work, three annotated before-and-after details,
and a **1000–1500 word illustrated commentary**. Include citations for the
historical reproduction, credit the generated course reference, and clearly
include your experiment sheet and label both student paintings with your own name, medium and date.

Bring the paintings or high-resolution images and a draft commentary to the
week-12 seminar for a **ten-minute live defense**: two minutes introducing your
choices, followed by questions. Answer at least one question about a specific
weakness in your visual comparison or attribution reasoning. Submit the finished
portfolio by the Friday deadline above, incorporating the seminar feedback.
Both portfolio and defense are required. Declare assistance according to the
[policies page](/policies/).
